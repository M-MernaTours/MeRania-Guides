What You Will See on Luxor's East Bank

The temples of Karnak and Luxor are part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site of Ancient Thebes with its Necropolis. Together they tell the story of more than a thousand years of royal building, religious festivals, and later history.

Karnak Temple — The Great Precinct of Amun-Re

Karnak is one of the largest religious complexes ever built, enlarged by generation after generation of pharaohs over many centuries. Enter past the avenue of ram-headed sphinxes and step into the Great Hypostyle Hall, a forest of 134 colossal sandstone columns in 16 rows, built largely under Seti I, with its decoration completed by Ramses II.

Your guide helps you read the carved reliefs, find the towering obelisks—including one raised by Queen Hatshepsut—and understand how the temple worked as the earthly home of Amun-Re.

Luxor Temple — Amenhotep III & Ramses II

In the center of modern Luxor, Luxor Temple was built mainly by Amenhotep III and enlarged by Ramses II, whose colossal statues and single remaining obelisk still stand before the entrance pylon. Its twin obelisk now stands in Paris, on the Place de la Concorde.

The temple was the focus of the great Opet Festival, when the sacred images of the Theban Triad—Amun, Mut, and Khonsu—traveled here in procession from Karnak. Later layers of history survive too, including the mosque of the Sufi sheikh Abu el-Haggag, which still stands within the temple.

The Avenue of Sphinxes

This ceremonial road once linked Luxor Temple and Karnak across roughly 2.7 kilometers. After decades of excavation and restoration, it officially reopened in November 2021, and you can walk part of it with your guide, depending on your time and site access on the day.

Flexible Timing & Route Order: Pickup time and the order of visits can be adjusted around your hotel, the season, and your preferred pace—many guests enjoy Luxor Temple in the softer late-afternoon light. Because official site opening hours vary by site and season, your guide plans the route so you have comfortable time at each temple.
