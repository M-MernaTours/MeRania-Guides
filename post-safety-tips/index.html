Egypt Travel Safety Tips for First-Time Visitors

Simple, realistic advice to help you travel around Cairo, Giza and the rest of Egypt comfortably and confidently.

Most trips to Egypt go smoothly, and a little preparation makes a real difference. The tips below come from our everyday experience guiding private tours. They are practical habits rather than rules, and they apply whether you travel independently or with a guide.

They are useful across many of our experiences, from a camel ride at the Pyramids and a felucca ride on the Nile to Luxor's East Bank and West Bank and the Fayoum desert.

Before You Travel

  • Documents: Keep your passport secure and store a photo or photocopy of your passport and visa separately. Identification may be requested at hotels, airports and some checkpoints, so keep your passport with you on travel days.
  • Travel insurance: Consider insurance that covers medical care and the activities you plan, such as desert trips or quad biking.
  • Official advice: Check your own government's official travel advice for Egypt before you book and again shortly before you leave.
  • Transport: Arrange airport transfers and day-trip transport in advance rather than accepting offers on arrival. See our private transportation options.

Meeting Your Guide and Getting Around

Confirm your pickup time, hotel name and a contact number the day before each tour, so you know who is meeting you and where. If a plan changes, message your tour contact directly rather than relying on someone who approaches you in the street or at your hotel entrance.

Use transport arranged through your hotel, a ride-hailing app or your tour company, and politely decline unsolicited offers of rides or "shortcuts". Allow realistic travel time: Cairo traffic can be heavy, especially at rush hour.

Belongings on a Private Tour

On our private tours, larger bags can stay in the air-conditioned vehicle while you explore, so you only need to carry the essentials. Keep your passport, money and phone with you, and check your seat and surroundings before leaving the vehicle at the end of the day.

At the Pyramids, Temples and Museums

Every archaeological site and museum has its own rules, and they can change. Signs at the entrance and staff on site have the final word.

  • Stay on visitor routes: Do not climb monuments or enter roped-off or closed areas, however tempting the photo.
  • Photography: Rules differ between sites. Some areas restrict photography or flash, and some require a separate permit, so check the signs or ask your guide.
  • Crowds and belongings: Busy entrances and popular viewpoints are where bags are most easily misplaced. A cross-body bag that closes securely is a good choice.
  • Camel, horse and photo offers: Agree on the price and what it includes before you start. A polite but firm "No, thank you" is enough if you are not interested.

Museums usually have their own entry procedures and bag rules. Our Giza Pyramids & Grand Egyptian Museum visitor guide covers timing and highlights for both sites.

Money, Bargaining and Shopping

  • Currency exchange: Use ATMs or official bank branches and exchange offices. Never hand cash to strangers who offer to exchange money in the street.
  • Agree on prices first: Street vendors and bazaar sellers often start high, and bargaining is normal in markets. Agree on the price before any service begins, and ask your guide what a reasonable price looks like.
  • Shopping is your choice: Shopping stops are never required on our tours, in line with our no forced shopping policy. If you buy souvenirs, ask for a receipt, which is useful if you need to raise a problem with the shop later.
  • Carry small notes: Small Egyptian pound notes make small purchases and tips easier and avoid change problems.

Tipping (Baksheesh)

Tipping is common in Egypt, but it is always a personal choice. As our booking policies state, tips and gratuities for your guide or driver are entirely optional, with no fixed amount and no required percentage.

  • Tip what feels right for the service you received, if you wish to.
  • If someone offers help you did not ask for and then expects a tip, you can decline politely.
  • Keeping a few small notes separate from the rest of your cash makes tipping simple and discreet.

Health and Comfort in the Heat

  • Sun protection: A hat, sunglasses and sunscreen are essential at open-air sites, even in winter.
  • Water: Drink regularly throughout the day, before you feel thirsty.
  • Footwear: Wear comfortable closed shoes; temple floors, desert paths and tomb steps can be uneven.
  • Clothing: Choose light, breathable layers, and bring a warmer layer for winter evenings or the desert.
  • Pace yourself: Take breaks in the shade and plan open-air sites for the morning or late afternoon in the warmer months.

Respectful Travel and Local Customs

  • Religious sites: At mosques and churches, cover shoulders and knees. Women should bring a headscarf for mosque visits, and access can be limited during prayers or services.
  • Photographing people: Always ask before taking photos of local people, and respect a refusal.
  • Local customs: Modest dress is appreciated in most public places, and a few words of Arabic, such as "shukran" (thank you), are always welcome.

How a Licensed Guide Can Help

No guide can remove every travel risk, but a knowledgeable licensed guide makes many everyday situations simpler. A guide can explain site procedures and photography rules, coordinate tickets and transport, suggest a comfortable pace and tell you what to expect before you arrive somewhere new.

Our tours are private, with a flexible pace and transparent arrangements, and you are never required to shop. Guide and language availability are confirmed when you book. Learn more about our team or browse our private day tours.

Quick Checklist

  • Passport secured, with a copy stored separately.
  • Pickup time, meeting point and tour contact number confirmed.
  • Small notes for tips and small purchases.
  • Water, a hat and sun protection for every sightseeing day.
  • Modest clothing and a headscarf for religious sites.
  • Prices agreed before any service begins.
  • Belongings checked before leaving vehicles and sites.
